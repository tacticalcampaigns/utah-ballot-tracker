By congressional district

District Registered (active) Ballots in results Δ last release Est. received Est. left to report Turnout
Est. left is built per county, the same method as the legislative districts: each CD’s expected total in a county is a blended share of that county’s estimated received (see the Closest Legislative Races page for the formula), minus what it has counted there — then the CDs in each county are scaled so they sum exactly to that county’s remaining pool. CDs use the registration share only (100% current): the 2025 congressional redraw changed CD boundaries, so 2022/2024 CD shares are for different districts and are not comparable — so the CD low–high range collapses to the point. “Est. received” is ballots in results + est. left (no county report exists at CD level). “Registered” is active registered voters as of Nov 3.

How to read this: same definitions as the Closest Legislative Races page. “Could still flip” means the trailing candidate’s needed share of the district’s estimated outstanding ballots is within a plausible range — estimated from the district’s partisan registration (active registered voters, Tableau voter file) plus how much that party’s vote over- or underperformed its registration in the congressional race in that county, with the range from how much that over/underperformance varied across the CD’s counties, and a county-weighted counted-share check (see the Closest Legislative Races page for the full method). “Expected to flip” means the expected share alone is enough. “Too close” is a margin under 2%. “Flipped” compares the leader’s party to the party that held that district number before the 2025 congressional redraw (Moore CD1, Maloy CD2, Kennedy CD3, Owens CD4) — the redraw moved the incumbents, so this is a district-number comparison, not same-boundaries. Party & vote choice by district

For each congressional district: who is registered there (by party) vs how the district actually voted. Registration from the Nov 3 snapshot; vote choice from the latest All-Results release.
How to read this section: each district has two charts side by side. On the left, the registration barometer: one bar per party, bar height = that party’s registered voters in the district, bar fully filled with the party color, labeled with the party’s share of the district’s registered voters. On the right, the vote-share pie: slices sized by raw votes cast for each candidate in that district’s U.S. House race. The table under them compares the two directly — registration share vs vote share — so the gap shows where unaffiliated voters broke and any crossover went. The statewide section at the top pairs two barometers instead: registration on the left, and the summed partisan vote choice across all four U.S. House races on the right, in the same chart form so the two compare directly. This is a registration-mix vs vote-mix comparison, not a turnout measure. It does not say how many registered Republicans, Democrats, or unaffiliated voters actually voted. The Nov 3 “voted” columns are not used anywhere on this page.
